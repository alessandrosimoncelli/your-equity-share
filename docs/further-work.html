Further work

What the model does not do, and what each omission is worth

Version 1.0, September 2026. Companion to the technical methodology, which describes what the model does do.

Every figure here is as of 9 September 2026 and is recomputed by the tools named beside it. Equity shares are for the default household of the methodology: age 45, 1,500,000 of financial wealth, 100,000 of salary, risk aversion 5, on the Italian configuration, which currently recommends 57.9%.

This is the list of things the model deliberately does not do. It exists so that finishing can mean finishing rather than forgetting, and so that anyone reading the tool can see the shape of what is missing rather than discovering it later.

Every entry carries a number, or says plainly that it does not have one yet. An omission with no measured cost is a wish rather than a plan, and it is impossible to prioritise a list of wishes. Where the number is absent it is because measuring it is itself part of the work.

1. The Italian variant

1.1 Apply the tax asymmetry to the answer

The Italian tax code is modelled in src/your_equity_share/taxes.py and its output is recorded in the configuration, but the headline recommendation is still computed before tax. The two after-tax figures sit in the provenance and nothing reads them.

It was left that way because the American variant is pre-tax. Applying tax on one side only would make the two answers differ by method as well as by country, which is the thing every other decision in this project has been made to avoid. Doing it properly means deciding what the American equivalent is, and United States tax depends on the account a household happens to hold the asset in, which Italian withholding does not.

Worth 4.6 points, 57.9% down to 53.4% at a thirty-year deferral.

The finding underneath it is worth keeping in view, because it is the opposite of what the rates suggest. It is not the 26% that discourages equities. Levy the same 26% on both sides and the recommendation rises to 66.1%, because the safe asset is taxed every year on its whole nominal coupon while equities defer to sale. What tilts an Italian household towards bonds is the preference given to government paper, and that preference is worth 12.7 points on its own.

1.2 The deferral horizon, taken from the household

An accumulating fund pays no tax until it is sold, so the equity tax is deferred, and how much that is worth depends on for how long. The model fixes it at thirty years to match the maturity of the safe asset.

That is the one genuinely free parameter the tax work introduced, and it is large: 10.6 points across ten to forty years. The household object already carries ages, and the model already knows a retirement age and a mortality table, so the parameter can be removed rather than tuned.

1.3 Pension wrappers

A third tax regime sits alongside the two the model now carries, and for a household that uses it, it is probably larger than everything else in this document. Italian pension funds are taxed on returns at 20% rather than 26%, with 12.5% on the government bond share; contributions are deductible from income up to 5,164.57 euro a year; and the benefit is taxed at 15%, falling by 0.3 points a year from the sixteenth year of membership to 9% after thirty-five.

It is deferred because it changes the asset menu rather than the rates. A household choosing between a fund inside a pension wrapper and the same fund outside one is answering a different question from the one this model asks, and the deductibility in particular interacts with income rather than with wealth.

1.4 Shares rather than funds

Gains on funds are redditi di capitale and losses on the same funds are minusvalenze, which are redditi diversi, and Italian law does not let the two be netted. Gains on individual shares are redditi diversi too, so there the losses do offset.

That distinction is why the model does not reduce the volatility for tax. With full loss offset the state has taken a stake in the position and shares the downside as well as the upside, which is the Domar and Musgrave (1944) result and would argue for holding more equity, not less. A household holding individual shares is in that regime and this model does not represent it.

1.5 The human capital calibration

This is the largest declared deviation of the Italian variant and the largest piece of work on this list. Cocco, Gomes and Maenhout's earnings profile, their permanent and temporary shock volatilities, and United States mortality are all fitted inside Choi's coefficients and cannot be changed from a configuration file. Only the benefit replacement rate could honestly be made Italian.

Estimating the Italian equivalents from the Bank of Italy's Survey on Household Income and Wealth would mean refitting the coefficients rather than substituting inputs, which is why it is not a configuration change.

1.6 Italian inflation rather than euro area

The safe rate is deflated by euro area HICP. An Italian household's cost of living is Italian, and only BTP Italia tracks that, at five years and retail. So the deflator is approximate in a way an American's TIPS is not.

1.7 Currency

The growth term is real growth in United States earnings per share, read as a euro real rate. That holds if relative purchasing power is preserved, which is AQR's own stated assumption for reading local real returns in any currency. It is defensible over a lifetime because real exchange rates mean-revert over decades. It is not defensible over five years, and nothing in the model represents the real currency losses a euro investor in an unhedged world fund can experience for long stretches.

A hedged share class would remove that risk at a cost, and the model has no way to express the trade.

1.8 Withholding tax

The dividend yield is measured gross, because Shiller's dividend column is gross and the two variants have to measure one thing one way. Withholding costs 0.34 points of yield, and an American holding domestic stock pays none of it. It is recorded rather than netted off, because netting a tax into a market return hides it inside a price.

1.9 The Italian methodology

The reasoning behind the Italian variant currently lives in configuration comments and commit messages. It needs the document the American variant has.

2. Both variants

2.1 The horizon audit of the peer table

Table 29 of the methodology sets this model against ten firms. Every one of them forecasts seven to twenty years and this model prices a lifetime, so the comparison is not like for like. The correction is noted there in prose and never carried into the number: Vanguard, the only firm publishing both horizons off one model run, has its thirty-year forecast 0.9 points above its ten-year.

2.2 A better global growth series

tools/global_growth.py is the record of trying to measure global real dividend growth from free long-horizon data and failing: the answer moves 4.5 points across weighting schemes that are equally defensible, because a fixed basket of countries is not an index and reconstitution cannot be reproduced from country index returns. If a series ever appears that carries the world index's own constituents, the growth term stops needing to be borrowed from the United States.

3. Not on this list, and why

Two things were considered and rejected rather than deferred, and they are recorded here so they do not come back as suggestions.

Historical average returns, for either the expected equity return or the safe rate. Choi does not use them: his stated rationale for the 5% default is current valuation ratios, held constant, with growth at its long-run historical average, which is the building block the model implements. The backtest rejects them: his flat 5% scores worse than every conditional estimator at every horizon. And the literature rejects them, most directly in Fama and French (2002), who show that estimates from dividend and earnings growth are far more precise than estimates from average returns, and that the high realised average of the second half of the twentieth century came from an unexpected decline in discount rates, which is a one-off capital gain rather than a repeatable expected return.

A more frequently published capital market assumption. AQR publish once a year and the houses that publish quarterly or monthly all assume valuations revert towards a fair value, which is a different estimator rather than a fresher number. Switching for the sake of frequency would change the method. The two halves of the estimate are checked against free data on every run instead.
